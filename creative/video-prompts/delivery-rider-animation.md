# Urban Delivery Rider — Shot-by-Shot Video Prompt

**Concept:** An inline delivery rider who never waits for the road to be completed. While skating at full speed, he activates location pins to unfold new roads, passes beneath a gigantic moving parcel, catches another package while still in motion, and delivers it without stopping. The rider continuously moves forward while the road, delivery objects, and the surrounding city physically react to his movement.

- **Total duration:** 12 seconds
- **Structure:** 12 shots, ~1 second per shot, each shot connecting directly to the next with no split-screen layouts
- **Motion style:** wheel rotation, strong skating pushes, low aerodynamic posture, carving, acceleration, and momentum — not repeated jumps or unrealistic aerial spins

---

## Reference Image Role

Use the attached master reference image for the character, inline skates, color palette, illustration style, urban environment, and final hero composition.

**Maintain throughout every shot:**
- Lime-green cap
- Bright orange windbreaker
- Deep navy wide pants
- White socks with black stripes
- Blue-and-white inline skate boots
- Lime-green wheels
- Black gloves
- Cream, black, and lime crossbody delivery bag
- Identical crossbody strap position and bag orientation in every shot

**Character rules:**
- Only one character exists
- Primarily shown from behind and rear three-quarter angles
- No unnecessary frontal facial close-ups

**Palette / style:**
- Saturated cobalt-blue sky, deep navy buildings, cream surfaces, bright orange clothing, lime-green accents
- Bold graphic illustration with angular cel-shaded planes, subtle paper grain, dramatic foreshortening
- Never photorealistic footage or glossy plastic-like 3D rendering

---

## Feet and Inline Skate Direction — Highest Priority

- The character always travels forward in the direction his body is facing
- Toe of each skate points toward the direction of travel; heel always remains behind the toe
- From a rear camera angle, the camera-facing part of the skate primarily shows the heel, rear frame, and rear wheels — never reverse the skate so the toe points at the camera while skating forward
- Left skate stays on the left leg, right skate on the right leg; each boot and wheel frame is one rigid structure; the foot never rotates independently below the ankle
- Wheels stay in a single straight row under each boot — never quad skates, never spread sideways
- **Propulsion:** support leg rolls forward while the opposite skate pushes diagonally backward and outward, then lifts and returns beside the support leg; the pushing skate always retains a forward-facing toe component — never twist it fully backward
- **Gliding:** both feet ~hip-width apart, one skate slightly ahead of the other
- **Curves:** hips, knees, boots, and wheels all follow the same turning arc — never keep knees forward while twisting only the feet sideways
- No cross-step cornering, no backward skating, no foot-tip spinning, no aerial skate rotation
- Wherever the reference image's perspective makes skate direction ambiguous, correct it into anatomically and mechanically believable forward inline skating

---

## World Design

Route progression (spatially continuous, never disconnected):
1. Narrow delivery alley
2. Unfolding elevated roadway
3. Low tunnel beneath a gigantic package
4. Building-side delivery section
5. Broad open urban boulevard under the sky

- Cream-colored road surface with deep navy structural sides, including foldable segments
- Activating a location pin unfolds road panels sequentially ahead of the rider; the road fully connects and locks before he reaches it — it is never removed beneath him and he never skates through empty space
- Floating delivery boxes travel along cyan-colored logistics routes; boxes stay rigid — only loose flaps and tape ends flex with airflow
- Location pins and chat-bubble graphics behave like elastic graphic objects: they compress, bend, or rebound only when physically touched or activated

## Shot List

### [0–1s] Shot 1 — Motion Begins at the Wheels
Extreme low rear three-quarter close-up, nearly touching the road. Lime-green inline wheels roll rapidly across the cream surface; the skate heel, rear wheels, and outside edge of the boot dominate the frame, toe pointing deeper into the frame toward the travel direction. As the wheels cross a small road seam, the contact surfaces compress slightly and recover. Camera travels at wheel speed — only the road markings streak backward. Forward motion is already active from frame one.

### [1–2s] Shot 2 — Powerful Push and Acceleration
Camera pulls back to reveal the full rider from behind. Right skate rolls forward as the support leg while the left skate pushes strongly backward and outward, then lifts and returns beside the right skate. Upper body lowers to cut drag; arms counterbalance the legs naturally. As speed increases, the crossbody bag lags slightly backward from inertia and the orange sleeves inflate briefly in the airflow before settling. The speed increase visibly matches the strength of the push.

### [2–3s] Shot 3 — Opening the Route While Moving
Rear-right waist-height tracking shot. Without stopping, the rider extends his right hand and presses a large lime-green location pin floating beside the road. The pin tilts in the travel direction and compresses slightly at the contact point before springing back. A cyan route line shoots rapidly forward from the pin. Both knees stay bent and both skates keep rolling through the reach — the arm's movement never rotates the hips or skates in the opposite direction.

### [3–4s] Shot 4 — The Folded Road Opens
High rear overhead angle. Following the cyan route line, folded elevated road segments open one after another, each rigid panel rotating around a visible hinge point and locking into place, forming a gently descending curve between buildings. Small vibrations travel through the support structures as each segment locks. The rider enters only after the road directly ahead has fully settled, placing both skates in a stable parallel glide and lowering his body further for speed. The camera holds both the rider and the newly formed route in one composition.

### [4–5s] Shot 5 — Carving Through the Curve
Low side-tracking camera outside the curve. The rider enters a broad banked turn at high speed, keeping one skate slightly ahead of the other without crossing his feet, leaning his whole body naturally into the curve. Hips, knees, boots, and wheels all follow the same arc. The crossbody bag swings outward from centrifugal inertia, pulling the strap taut. Foreground barriers sweep past rapidly while distant buildings move slower for parallax. Skate silhouettes stay readable despite background motion blur.

### [5–6s] Shot 6 — Diving Into the Alley
Rear three-quarter tracking shot, camera dropping lower as the elevated roadway funnels down into a narrow delivery alley. Deep navy building walls close in on both sides; cream awnings and stacked crates flick past in the foreground, creating tight parallax layers. The rider narrows his stance and pulls his elbows in, skates tracking in a straight, tight line beneath him. A cyan logistics route glows faintly overhead, threading between the buildings and guiding his path. Speed reads through streaking wall textures rather than any change in posture height.

### [6–7s] Shot 7 — The Gigantic Package Approaches
Rear low-angle shot. Ahead, an enormous delivery parcel — cream cardboard with black tape seams and a lime shipping label — glides slowly along its own cyan route line, suspended above the road and spanning the full width of the alley. The rider drops into a deep crouch, lowering his center of gravity, both skates still rolling forward in a stable parallel glide. The gap beneath the package narrows into frame as he commits to passing underneath it. Loose tape ends on the parcel's corner flutter gently in the airflow.

### [7–8s] Shot 8 — Passing Beneath the Parcel
Extreme low rear-following shot, camera pressed near the ground. The rider's cap and shoulders pass just beneath the shadowed underside of the giant package, the navy shadow briefly darkening the cream road surface. Both skates stay perfectly parallel and rolling, knees bent low, arms tucked tight to the body for clearance. As he emerges from beneath the parcel, sunlight returns across the road in a sharp graphic transition and his posture begins to rise back upright.

### [8–9s] Shot 9 — Catching the Second Package
Rear-right three-quarter tracking shot at shoulder height. A second, smaller box travels along a cyan route line converging with the rider's path. Without slowing, he extends his left arm and catches it against his side, the box compressing slightly into his forearm and the crossbody bag on impact before settling into a secure carry. Both skates remain in continuous rolling motion throughout the catch — the support leg glide never breaks. The rider's torso absorbs the momentum with a brief forward lean.

### [9–10s] Shot 10 — Building-Side Delivery
Rear-left tracking shot alongside a row of navy building facades with cream delivery hatches. Still moving at speed, the rider swings the newly caught box toward a lime-rimmed delivery hatch set into the wall. The hatch's chat-bubble-style indicator compresses and lights up on contact, then rebounds as the box locks into place inside it. The rider's skates never stop rolling — one skate stays the support leg while the delivery arm extends and retracts in one continuous motion, hips staying square to the direction of travel.

### [10–11s] Shot 11 — Breaking Into the Open Boulevard
Wide rear three-quarter shot as the alley opens outward into a broad urban boulevard beneath the saturated cobalt sky. The rider straightens up from his delivery lean, both skates gliding side by side, and pushes once more — support leg rolling forward as the opposite skate drives back and out — to build speed into the open space. Deep navy buildings recede into the distance on either side, and distant cyan route lines crisscross the sky above the boulevard. The road widens visibly in front of him, fully connected and locked.

### [11–12s] Shot 12 — Hero Composition, Full Speed Ahead
Final rear three-quarter hero shot, matching the reference image's signature composition. The rider is fully upright in a low aerodynamic glide, both skates aligned hip-width apart with one slightly forward, wheels blurring beneath him against the cream boulevard surface. The crossbody bag sits steady in its identical orientation, orange windbreaker catching the airflow, lime cap steady. The cyan route line stretches ahead of him into the distance beneath the cobalt sky, open and fully unfolded, as he continues forward without slowing — the motion carrying past the final frame rather than resolving into a stop.

- [ ] Road is always fully connected before the rider reaches each new segment
- [ ] No cross-stepping, backward skating, foot-tip spinning, or aerial skate rotation in any shot
- [ ] Speed changes always driven by visible pushes/carving, not by camera tricks alone
- [ ] Color palette (cobalt sky, navy buildings, cream surfaces, orange/lime character, cyan routes) held constant
- [ ] Illustration style (angular cel-shading, paper grain, dramatic foreshortening) held constant — no photorealism, no glossy 3D
